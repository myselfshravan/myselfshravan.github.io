The company

What Klydo was

Klydo set out to make fashion feel as instant and low-risk as it should be for people who grew up online. Two ideas made it distinctive: get the clothes to your door the same day, and let you try them on at home and pay only for what you keep. Underneath a playful shopping app sat a real quick-fulfillment operation and a real consumer-credit engine.

⚡ Same-day fashion

One dark store, geofenced delivery zones and slotted routing put most orders on the doorstep in under two hours fast for fashion, without pretending to be 15-minute grocery.

Median 1.86 h · 52% under 2 h · 99.7% same-day

💳 Try-Now-Pay-Later

Receive it, try it, pay if you keep it. A great customer promise and a hard risk problem backed by automated underwriting and an LLM fraud-review pipeline with human review for the edge cases.

276,045 users approved for credit · 331K underwriting checks

🧩 Server-driven app

Home, cart and order screens were rendered from backend-defined components (SDUI) so merchandising, multi-cart and even an iOS home-screen widget shipped without app releases.

30+ SDUI components · Flutter + Kotlin

My contribution

What I built

I was a founding-era engineer on a small core team full-stack across a Kotlin/Ktor backend and a Flutter app, for about six months through the company's fastest-growing period. I wasn't the highest-volume committer, and I'd rather be honest about that: what I owned, I owned end-to-end. Roughly 131 merged production changes across the two repos.

Flagship · TNPL credit engine

Sherlock the agent that opened up credit

For months, Try-Now-Pay-Later was a members-only privilege gated by a static risk score, because manual review didn't scale. I built Sherlock: an AI order-evaluation agent a 24-signal collector, a scoring engine, and an LLM fraud reviewer that decides which orders ship on credit and routes only the genuinely ambiguous ones to a human. On one morning in April 2026 we deleted the old gate and opened TNPL to every user. Fraud stayed flat; the admin chase queue emptied.

Growth & conversion I shipped
Basket-building

🎯 D2R "spend ₹X more, unlock ₹Y off"

A cart-level Distance-to-Reward banner showing live progress toward the next coupon unlock turning "add one more thing" into a visible, satisfying goal. I designed the progress model and shipped it across backend and app. It became one of the most direct levers on basket size.

~₹900 → ~₹1,100average order value lifted · ≈ +22%
Conversion nudges

⏳ Urgency construct scarcity, done honestly

Low-stock urgency badges ("only a few left") wired into the cart and product cards, driven by real reservation-aware inventory not fake countdowns. A small, honest nudge that pushed hesitant carts over the line without eroding trust, tunable by a stock threshold.

The big one

🎁 Referral a growth loop, end to end

The referral system was the largest thing I owned. Deep-link capture with auto-apply share a link, the code lands in your cart with zero typing plus an earnings wallet, leaderboard, and post-return re-evaluation so rewards stayed honest. Frontend and backend, mine end-to-end, and a real low-friction acquisition channel.

47,318referral codes generated · 3,264 rewards paid

The earnings wallet that referral and cashback paid into balances, transaction history, and a real bank/UPI withdrawal flow, including the reconciliation fixes that correctly handled failed payouts and payout amounts. At shutdown it held a fully-accounted balance owed, ready to settle cleanly.

The ending

How it finally ended

On 8 July 2026 Klydo stopped taking new orders. There was no dramatic outage the wind-down was engineered as carefully as a launch. The backend began answering the consumer app with a polite 410 Gone, while admin tools, webhooks and health checks kept running so order history and support stayed available. The app itself showed a farewell screen.

"We've stopped taking new orders on Klydo, effective today. The app will remain available for the next few days for order history, support, or anything else you need. Klydo is pivoting in a new direction based on everything we've learned so far… This isn't goodbye. It's the beginning of what's next."
the in-app shutdown notice

🧾 Wound down responsibly

A full source-backed shutdown archive was produced: the customer identity graph, order history, wallet ledger and segmentation with a data-retention and privacy plan. Outstanding wallet balances (₹69,457 across 283 users) flagged to be settled before the entity closes.

🌱 Not the end of the story

The consumer business paused, but the hard parts a credit/underwriting stack, an SDUI engine, catalog connectors and a clean identity graph are the reusable foundation the company is carrying into its new direction.

Not every company continues, and this one is pivoting. What stays with me isn't the outcome it's what it felt like to ship a credit product from scratch, to get a 2 a.m. page and fix it, and to watch a referral flow you wrote quietly become a real acquisition channel. On to what's next.
